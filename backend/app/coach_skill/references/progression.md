# Driver progression and practice method

Use this to decide *what to coach first*. A fix the driver isn't ready for wastes their limited attention.
Paraphrased from Suellio Almeida's development order; levels are a guide, not a gate.

## Level 1 — safety and consistency
1. **Vision**: plan ~0.5–1 s ahead (not further), with quick glances at the car's position to check.
   No racing-line aid. Symptoms of weak vision: late, then rushed turn-ins; inconsistent brake points.
2. **Entry speed control**: same reference, same peak pressure, same gear and RPM at turn-in, every lap.
3. **Entry track usage**: actually reach the outside edge before braking (verify on replay).
4. **Feeling and inducing understeer**: use it to measure how fast each corner can be taken.

## Level 2 — balance and speed
5. Light hands / steering force.
6. Feeling and inducing oversteer.
7. Trail braking and string theory (basic → advanced).
8. Throttle application (initial application → modulation).
9. Engine-braking control (downshift timing as dynamic brake bias).
10. Downforce awareness (peak vs terminal braking, U vs V lines).
11. Elevation and camber.

## Level 3 — precision
12. Brake bias (worth tenths only once everything above is solid).
13. Exponential vs linear steering.
14. Neutral-steer control from the first moment of turn-in.
15. Track knowledge (bumps, surfaces, kerbs).
16. Setup knowledge.

## How to place a driver *(engineering note)*
- Lap-to-lap spread > ~0.5 s, unstable entry speeds → Level 1 work first.
- Consistent but ~1 s+ off, release-then-steer, high active-countersteer count, MRL plateaus → Level 2.
- Within a few tenths, clean traces, issues in neutral-steer duration or MRP placement → Level 3.
- Plateau archetypes:
  - **Careful-consistent** (laps within tenths, never past the limit, long post-slide hesitation):
    deliberately overdrive *slow* corners to get comfortable correcting; keep max rotation after a slip.
  - **Aggressive-sloppy** (brakes too late, lots of slides, recovers fast): brake a hair earlier and
    focus force on late entry/early exit rather than early entry.
  Both: force rotation toward the middle of the corner before chasing later braking.

## Practice method
- ~5 % learning new ideas, ~95 % repeating them until automatic; then let them sink and add the next layer.
- One or two conscious focuses per session. No blind laps.
- Test a new technique by exaggerating it, giving other inputs time to adapt, then refining.
- Rotation first, speed after: find the rotation limit at a safe speed before moving brake points.
- Learn in slow corners first, then medium, then fast.
- "Refresh rate": beginners sense one state per corner, intermediates two (e.g. understeer → snap), experts
  four or more (early entry / late entry / early exit / late exit). Push the driver to name states per phase.
- Equipment: with low-torque wheels, set FFB so it clips only where detail matters least (compressions);
  with long pedal dead travel, calibrate so full travel maps to the useful brake range.
